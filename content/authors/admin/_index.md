Lei Zhao is a Professor and PhD advisor at the College of Resources and Environmental Sciences, China Agricultural University. His research focuses on theoretical ecology, particularly the diversity and stability of ecosystems and food webs, ecosystem responses to global change, and the structure and function of ecological networks. He has published over 50 SCI-indexed papers, including 17 as first or corresponding author in journals such as *Nature Communications*, *Nature Ecology & Evolution*, *Nature Climate Change*, *Ecology Letters*, and *Journal of Animal Ecology*.

He serves on the Ecological Modeling Committee of the Ecological Society of China, as an editorial board member of *Chinese Journal of Plant Ecology*, a young editorial board member of *Chinese Journal of Applied Ecology*, an Associate Editor of *Methods in Ecology and Evolution*, and a Guest Editor of *Ecosphere*. He also reviews for *Nature Communications*, *Ecology Letters*, *Ecology*, and other journals. He has led six research projects at the provincial and ministerial level, including a sub-project of the National Key R&D Program of China, General Program and Young Scientists Fund grants from the National Natural Science Foundation of China, and grants from the Beijing Municipal Natural Science Foundation. He was selected for a national-level young talent program.

### Research Areas

1. **Biodiversity and community stability** — mechanisms maintaining community stability and how diversity affects it (representative paper: Zhao et al., *Nature Communications*, 2022)
2. **Complex networks and food webs** — food-web dynamics and identifying keystone species using network theory (Zhao et al., *Ecology Letters*, 2016)
3. **Climate change impacts on ecosystems** — effects of warming on the structure and function of aquatic communities (O'Gorman & Zhao et al., *Nature Ecology & Evolution*, 2023; *Nature Climate Change*, 2017)
4. **Spatiotemporal distribution and Taylor's power law** — synchrony of population and community time series across spatial locations (Zhao et al., *Journal of Animal Ecology*, 2019)
